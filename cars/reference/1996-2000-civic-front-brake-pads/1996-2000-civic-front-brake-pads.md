# 1996–2000 Honda Civic Front Brake Pad Replacement

Front pad service on the 1996–2000 Civic restores bite, reduces rotor scoring, and keeps brake bias predictable. The job is straightforward, but sloppy hardware service is what causes noise and tapered pad wear.

## Applicability

- Honda Civic **1996–2000** (EK-generation in most markets) with front discs
- Confirm pad shape for trim and market (DX/LX/EX/Si and regional variants)

## Parts, Tools, and Consumables

- Front pads matched to the exact application
- Hardware kit (abutment clips) if originals are rusty or bent
- Brake cleaner, wire brush, high-temp grease rated for caliper slides
- C-clamp or piston compressor, torque wrench, jack stands, wheel chocks
- Optional: rotors, slide-pin boots, brake fluid for top-up only

## Safety

> [!WARNING]
> Support the vehicle on jack stands. Wet-clean brake dust - do not blow it into the air. Watch the master-cylinder level when retracting pistons so the reservoir does not overflow.

## Inspection

1. Measure rotor thickness and compare to the minimum stamped on the hat.
2. Check for heat cracks, hard spots, and excessive runout (pedal pulse).
3. Inspect hoses for cracks and caliper boots for fluid seepage.
4. Confirm slide pins move freely; seized pins cause inner/outer uneven wear.

## Removal

1. Chock the rear wheels, loosen the front lug nuts, raise and support the car, remove the wheel.
2. Remove the lower caliper guide bolt and swing the caliper up, or remove both bolts if required.
3. Support the caliper with wire. **Do not** let it hang from the hose.
4. Remove pads, shims, and clips. Note inner versus outer and any wear-indicator position.

## Service and Installation

1. Clean the caliper bracket abutments to bare, smooth contact areas.
2. Replace damaged clips. Lightly grease only the designated contact points - never the pad friction face.
3. Service slide pins: clean, inspect boots, grease with the correct synthetic caliper grease, and reinstall.
4. Compress the piston evenly using a wood block or compressor while monitoring fluid level.
5. Install new pads and shims in the original orientation.
6. Refit the caliper, torque guide-pin bolts to the factory specification, and reinstall the wheel.
7. Torque lug nuts in a star pattern to the published value.

## Before You Drive

1. Pump the brake pedal until it is firm.
2. Verify the parking brake still holds (rear system), then perform a low-speed stop test in a safe area.
3. Bed the pads per the pad manufacturer: typically a series of moderate decelerations without coming to a complete stop each cycle, then a cool-down drive.

## Common Failures After DIY

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| :--- | :--- |
| Squeal | Missing shims/clips, no abutment grease, cheap pads |
| Pull | Seized slide pin or contaminated pad face |
| Soft first stop | Pedal not pumped after piston retract |
| Vibration | Rotors below spec or unevenly worn |

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Pedal firm before moving
- [ ] No fluid leaks
- [ ] Even braking, no pull
- [ ] Lug nuts and guide pins torqued
- [ ] Bedding completed
