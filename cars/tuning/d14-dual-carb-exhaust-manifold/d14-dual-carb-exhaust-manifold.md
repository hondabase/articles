# D14 Dual-Carb Exhaust Manifold Selection (4-2-1 versus 4-1)

Choosing an exhaust manifold for a dual-carb **D14** (and similar low-power dual-carb D-series) is a street compromise between **torque shape**, **ground clearance**, **weight**, and **cost**. This assumes the car **stays carbureted**.

## Why change the manifold?

The stock cast manifold is heavy and restrictive. A tubular header reduces backpressure and thermal mass. Gains are modest on a stock D14 compared with a built engine, but they pair well with a free-flow system and a clean dual-carb tune.

## 4-2-1 versus 4-1

| Design | Street behaviour | Packaging | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4-2-1** | Stronger low/mid torque | Usually better clearance | Daily dual-carb D14 |
| **4-1** | Favours top-end flow | Collectors often hang lower | Higher-rpm cams / track bias |

For a mostly stock dual-carb 1.4, **4-2-1** is the usual recommendation.

## Product tiers (community experience)

- **Budget eBay tubular 4-2-1:** functional; weld quality varies - **die-grind the inside of the runners** to smooth weld penetration when needed
- **Megan-style 4-2-1:** same general runner layout as many budget units, usually cleaner welds
- **Name-brand 4-2-1 (e.g. 5Zigen-type):** different runner layout; one dual-carb owner preferred it for **low-end** and noted the car also ran **quieter** than expected - some units need **reinforcement welds** where thin tubing cracks under vibration

Always verify flange fitment for **D-series** dual-carb heads and collector diameter for the rest of the exhaust. Stock cast manifolds are heavy and restrictive; even a cheap tubular unit is considered worthwhile on dual-carb D14s by owners who have run several brands.

## Fitting notes (4-2-1 on a 1.4 GL)

- Two-piece headers are easier: fit the upper runners first, then join the collector
- Check crossmember, radiator support, and engine-mount clearance before final torquing
- Stock mid-pipe can sometimes mate with an adapter or slip joint; expect measurement and possible custom section
- Fix all exhaust leaks before trusting wideband AFR

## Pipe diameter after the header

For a near-stock dual-carb D14:

- **\~1.75 in (44 mm)** system diameter is a sensible street starting point
- **2 in (51 mm)** can feel hollow low down unless the engine flows more (cam, head work, open induction with proper fueling)

Pair the header with a free-flow mid and rear section; a header into a crushed stock system wastes the upgrade.

## Tuning after the header

A freer exhaust leans the mixture if the carb was barely adequate before. After installation:

1. Check for leaks
2. Re-balance carbs and idle mixture
3. Log cruise and WOT AFR
4. Adjust mixture screws first; consider main jets only with data

## Related power package (still carbed)

1. Service + rebuilt dual carbs
2. Airbox flap/heat delete + panel filter
3. **4-2-1 header + free-flow exhaust**
4. Wideband + mixture/balance
5. Optional mild timing advance
