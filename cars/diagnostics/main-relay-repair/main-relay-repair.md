# Honda Main Relay Diagnostic & Repair Guide

A faulty PGM-FI Main Relay is one of the most common causes of intermittent crank/no-start conditions in Honda and Acura vehicles produced between 1988 and 2000. This issue typically surfaces on hot days or after the engine has been sitting for a short period after driving.

---

## Symptoms of a Failing Main Relay

*   **Engine cranks but will not start:** The starter turns the engine over normally, but the fuel pump does not prime.
*   **No check engine light bulb check:** When turning the ignition key to Position II (ON), the Check Engine Light (CEL) does not illuminate for 2 seconds and then click off.
*   **No fuel pump prime sound:** You do not hear the 2-second fuel pump "hum" from the fuel tank when turning the key to Position II.
*   **Heat-sensitive failure:** The car starts fine in the morning, but fails to start after parking under direct sunlight.

## Step-by-Step Diagnostic Test

1.  Turn key to **Position II (ON)**.
2.  Listen closely for three distinct clicks:
    *   **Click 1:** Ignition switch energizes the main relay coil.
    *   **Click 2:** Fuel pump turns ON (humming sound for 2 seconds).
    *   **Click 3:** Fuel pump turns OFF (CEL turns off).
3.  If Click 2 or 3 is missing and the fuel pump does not hum, the main relay board has cracked solder joints.

---

## Repair Procedure: Solder Joint Reflow

Instead of replacing the main relay with an aftermarket unit (which often fails prematurely), reflowing the factory Mitsuba relay PCB solder joints provides a permanent repair.

> [!TIP]
> The original factory relay uses unleaded solder that oxidizes and cracks under heat cycles. Using 60/40 rosin-core solder resolves this permanently.

### Required Tools
*   Soldering Iron (30W–60W)
*   60/40 Rosin-Core Solder
*   Desoldering braid or suction pump
*   Flathead screwdriver (to remove relay plastic casing)

### Procedure
1.  Unplug the main relay connector and remove the mounting bolt (10mm).
2.  Gently pry open the plastic clips securing the circuit board inside the housing.
3.  Inspect the copper solder pads on the back of the circuit board under magnification. You will notice tiny ring cracks around the large relay terminal pins.
4.  Apply flux and touch the hot soldering iron tip to each cracked joint.
5.  Add fresh solder to form a smooth, shiny solder cone over all 7-8 primary pins.
6.  Reassemble the relay, reinstall it in the vehicle, and test fuel pump priming.
