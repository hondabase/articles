# Identifying Honda B-Series Manual Transmissions

Proper identification of a Honda B-series manual transmission is crucial for maintenance, repairs, and performance upgrades. Different B-series transmissions feature varying gear ratios, final drives, and differential types, which significantly impact vehicle performance and compatibility. This article outlines the primary methods for accurately identifying these transmissions.

## Locating the Transmission Case Code

The most reliable method for identifying a B-series transmission is by locating its case code. This alphanumeric code is typically cast or stamped directly onto the transmission housing.

* **Location:** The code is usually found on the bellhousing portion of the transmission, often near the clutch slave cylinder mounting points or on a flat surface adjacent to the shift linkage mechanism. It may also be visible on the top or front face of the transmission case.
* **Format:** The code usually consists of two or three characters (e.g., "S80", "Y21", "YS1").

> [!TIP]
> Cleaning the transmission case with a degreaser and a wire brush may be necessary to reveal obscured codes, especially on older or heavily used units.

## Common B-Series Transmission Codes

While specific gear ratios and final drives should always be confirmed with a factory service manual, certain case codes are commonly associated with particular B-series engine applications and features.

| Code | Common Associated Engine/Model (General) | Clutch Actuation | Common Features (General) |
| :--- | :--------------------------------------- | :--------------- | :------------------------ |
| **YS1** | Early B16A (JDM), B18A/B (USDM) | Cable | Rod shift linkage, typically open differential |
| **S4C** | B16A (JDM/USDM), B18C1 (USDM GSR) | Hydraulic | Often 4.4 final drive, optional limited-slip differential (LSD) |
| **Y21** | B16A (JDM) | Hydraulic | Similar to S4C, often 4.4 final drive |
| **S80** | B18C5 (USDM Integra Type R), B16B (JDM Civic Type R) | Hydraulic | Often includes factory LSD, 4.785 final drive (Type R) |
| **Y80** | B18C1 (USDM Integra GSR) | Hydraulic | Typically open differential, 4.4 final drive |
| **P80** | B20B/Z (CRV) | Hydraulic | Taller gearing, typically open differential, 4.062 final drive |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The information in this table is for general guidance. Always confirm the exact specifications, including gear ratios, final drive, and differential type, by cross-referencing the transmission's case code with a Honda factory service manual specific to the vehicle it originated from.

## Secondary Identification Methods: Visual Cues

In addition to case codes, several visual characteristics can help narrow down the identity of a B-series transmission.

### Bellhousing Design

* **Clutch Actuation:** Early B-series transmissions (e.g., YS1) utilize a **cable-actuated clutch** system, identifiable by the presence of a clutch fork designed for a cable. Later transmissions (e.g., S4C, Y80, S80) use a **hydraulic clutch** system, featuring a mounting point for a hydraulic slave cylinder.

### Shift Linkage Type

* **Rod vs. Cable:** Most B-series transmissions use a cable-style shift linkage. However, the YS1 transmission is notable for its **rod-style shift linkage**, which is visibly different from the cable mechanisms.

### Speed Sensor Type

* **Location and Design:** The Vehicle Speed Sensor (VSS) location and design can vary. Older transmissions might use a mechanical VSS, while newer ones employ an electronic sensor. Confirming the VSS type can sometimes help differentiate between generations or specific models.

### Differential Type

* **LSD Inspection:** If the transmission is out of the vehicle, it may be possible to visually inspect the differential through the axle openings or by removing the drain plug. An open differential will allow one output shaft to spin freely while holding the other, whereas an LSD will typically cause both shafts to rotate together or offer significant resistance.

## Confirming Transmission Specifications

For definitive information regarding gear ratios, final drive, and specific applications, always refer to the official Honda factory service manual (FSM) for the vehicle the transmission was originally installed in. If possible, obtaining the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) of the donor vehicle can greatly assist in cross-referencing parts catalogs for precise specifications.

> [!CAUTION]
> Never assume compatibility or specifications based solely on visual inspection or general knowledge. Incorrect transmission selection can lead to poor performance, damage to drivetrain components, or unsafe driving conditions.
