# Honda B-Series Manual Transmission Cleaning and Case Resealing

## Introduction
Over time, the factory sealant between the case halves of Honda B-series manual transmissions can degrade, leading to oil leaks. These leaks not only create a mess but can also result in insufficient lubrication if left unaddressed. This guide outlines the advanced procedure for thoroughly cleaning and resealing the transmission case, restoring its integrity and preventing further fluid loss.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This procedure requires significant mechanical aptitude, specialized tools, and a clean working environment. Referencing the official Honda factory service manual (FSM) for your specific vehicle and transmission model is crucial for accurate torque specifications, sealant recommendations, and detailed disassembly/assembly sequences.

## Tools and Materials

### Essential Tools
* **Socket Set and Wrenches:** Metric sizes (8mm, 10mm, 12mm, 14mm, 17mm, 19mm, etc.)
* **Torque Wrench:** Calibrated for both low and high torque values.
* **Pry Bars/Gasket Scrapers:** Non-marring types preferred.
* **Wire Brushes/Scrub Pads:** For cleaning old sealant.
* **Precision Pick Set:** For removing old sealant from grooves.
* **Clean Rags/Shop Towels:** Lint-free.
* **Parts Washer or Degreaser:** For thorough cleaning.
* **Safety Glasses and Gloves.**
* **Transmission Jack or Lift Table:** For safe handling.
* **Drain Pan.**

### Required Materials
* **Transmission Fluid:** Honda Genuine MTF or equivalent, as specified by the FSM.
* **High-Quality Liquid Gasket/Sealant:** HondaBond or an equivalent anaerobic or RTV sealant designed for oil resistance and transmission cases. Confirm type with FSM.
* **Brake Cleaner or Solvent:** For final surface preparation.
* **Optional:** Replacement seals (e.g., input shaft seal, output shaft seals) if they are also leaking or show signs of wear.

## Transmission Removal and Preparation

### Draining Transmission Fluid
1. Position a drain pan beneath the transmission.
2. Locate and remove the **drain plug** (typically a 1/2-inch square drive or 17mm bolt) on the bottom of the transmission case. Allow all fluid to drain completely.
3. Reinstall the drain plug with a new crush washer and tighten to factory specifications (consult FSM).

### Exterior Cleaning
* Thoroughly clean the exterior of the transmission with a degreaser and stiff brush. Removing all dirt, grease, and old oil will prevent contamination during disassembly and aid in identifying any other potential leak points.

### Transmission Removal
* Follow the factory service manual procedure for safely removing the transmission from the vehicle. This typically involves disconnecting linkages, sensors, drive axles, and supporting the engine.

> [!CAUTION]
> Transmissions are heavy. Always use appropriate lifting equipment and ensure the transmission is securely supported during removal and handling to prevent injury or damage.

## Disassembly of Transmission Case

### Separating Case Halves
1. Place the transmission on a clean, stable workbench or stand.
2. Remove all external components that connect the two case halves (e.g., shift forks, shift selector mechanism, speed sensor, reverse light switch, clutch slave cylinder bracket, etc.).
3. Carefully remove all bolts securing the two transmission case halves together. Note the location and length of each bolt, as they may vary.
4. Using non-marring pry tools, gently separate the case halves. Work slowly and evenly around the perimeter to avoid damaging the mating surfaces or internal components.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not force the case halves apart. If they are stuck, double-check that all bolts have been removed and gently tap with a rubber mallet. Internal components, particularly bearings and shafts, can be damaged if the cases are separated improperly.

### Internal Component Handling
* Once the case halves are separated, carefully lift the main shaft and countershaft assemblies out of the case. Keep all gears, synchronizers, and bearings together as an assembly.
* Place all internal components on a clean surface, organized to ensure correct reassembly. Taking photos during disassembly can be helpful.

## Cleaning and Inspection

### Case Halves Cleaning
1. Thoroughly remove all traces of old sealant from both mating surfaces of the transmission case halves. Use a gasket scraper, precision picks, and a wire brush. Be extremely careful not to scratch or gouge the aluminum surfaces.
2. Clean the case halves in a parts washer or with a strong degreaser to remove all oil residue, metal shavings, and cleaning agents. Rinse thoroughly.
3. Perform a final wipe-down of the mating surfaces with brake cleaner or a similar solvent to ensure they are perfectly clean and dry before sealant application.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Any remaining old sealant or oil residue will compromise the adhesion of the new sealant, leading to future leaks. The surfaces must be meticulously clean and dry.

### Component Inspection
* While the transmission is apart, inspect all internal components for wear, pitting, or damage. This includes gears, synchronizers, bearings, and shift forks. Replace any components that show excessive wear or damage.
* Inspect the input shaft and output shaft seals for leaks or hardening. Consider replacing these as a preventative measure while the transmission is disassembled.

## Resealing and Reassembly

### Sealant Application
1. Apply a continuous, even bead of the recommended liquid gasket/sealant to one of the clean, dry mating surfaces. Follow the sealant manufacturer's instructions regarding bead size and application method.
2. Ensure the sealant bead completely encircles all bolt holes and internal oil passages. Avoid applying too much sealant, as excess can squeeze into the transmission internals.

> [!TIP]
> Practice applying a consistent bead on a piece of cardboard first to ensure an even application when it matters.

### Case Half Reassembly
1. Carefully place the main shaft and countershaft assemblies back into position in one case half.
2. Align the second case half precisely over the first, ensuring that all dowel pins engage correctly and internal components mesh without binding. Gently lower the second half into place.
3. Install all case bolts by hand until finger-tight. Do not fully tighten yet.

### Torque Specifications
1. Consult the factory service manual for the specific torque sequence and values for the transmission case bolts. Honda transmissions often require a specific tightening pattern to ensure even pressure and proper sealing.
2. Tighten the bolts in several stages, following the FSM's specified pattern, until the final torque value is reached.
3. Allow the sealant to cure for the time specified by the sealant manufacturer before refilling with fluid or operating the transmission. This is typically several hours to 24 hours.

## Installation and Verification

### Transmission Installation
* Reinstall the transmission into the vehicle, reversing the removal procedure. Ensure all linkages, sensors, and mounts are correctly reconnected and torqued to specifications.

### Fluid Refill
1. Locate the **fill plug** on the transmission (typically on the front or side, often a 17mm or 23mm bolt).
2. Using a funnel and hose, slowly add the specified amount and type of transmission fluid until it reaches the bottom edge of the fill hole.
3. Reinstall the fill plug with a new crush washer and tighten to factory specifications.

### Leak Check
* After installation and fluid refill, start the engine and allow it to run for a short period. Visually inspect the transmission case for any signs of leaks around the newly sealed seam.
* Perform a short test drive and re-inspect for leaks. Monitor fluid levels over the next few days of operation.

> [!NOTE]
> A small amount of sealant squeeze-out is normal and indicates proper adhesion. However, any active dripping or seeping indicates an issue with the resealing process.
